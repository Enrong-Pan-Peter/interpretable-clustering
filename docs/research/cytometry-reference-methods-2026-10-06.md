## What should count as the reference?

For a readable marker-defined population, the practical reference is **expert serial gating on named measurements**, usually successive biaxial plots, with documented parent populations and gate boundaries. Recent primary work still treats that as the usual workflow and emphasizes translating discovered populations into physical sorting strategies. A population can depend on more than two markers overall even when every individual gate uses two. [ConvexGating, 18 September 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-77360-z).

For evaluation, use the more careful phrase **expert-annotated reference populations**, not unquestionable biological ground truth. FlowCAP deliberately tested recovery of expert gates separately from prediction of external sample-level outcomes. Matching a reference tells us agreement with those annotations; it does not prove that all unannotated or newly discovered populations are meaningless. [FlowCAP, 10 February 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/pmid/23396282/).

There are therefore three separate evidence targets: agreement with established populations; a short, executable marker definition; and independent biological support. Ideally the last includes another measurement, functional response, or experimental validation. The standard is not “highest ARI wins,” nor is FlowSOM or PhenoGraph a substitute for an expert reference. A claim of improvement over humans would require a relevant multi-operator comparison or independent validation, not one algorithm-versus-one-annotation score.

This directly answers transcript **00:20–01:28**: a method that proposes and verifies good two-marker gates can be worthwhile without requiring three-dimensional visualization. Three-marker candidates should earn their extra complexity empirically; larger panels remain an explicit-rule option.

## Closest methods, separated by their actual input task

Dates below refer to the cited paper's publication year or stated online/issue date, not a claim about the current software version. Reported evidence is the authors' evaluation scope, not reproduced here.

| Method and primary source | Input and output | Evidence, limitation and role here |
|---|---|---|
| **cytometree**, online 2 October 2018. [Paper](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/cyto.a.23601) | Unlabeled cells; recursively compares one- and two-component distributions for individual markers, with a stopping threshold; returns a binary tree and phenotype annotation. | Evaluated on FlowCAP and HIPC T-cell data, including 115 and 60 samples respectively, using agreement/F-measure against manual populations. Closest simple direct tree baseline. Marginal modality assumptions limit purely joint structures; the authors' favorable results are not a universal ranking. |
| **FAUST**, December 2021. [Paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8672150/), [author code](https://github.com/RGLab/FAUST) | Multiple experimental samples, marker measurements and design settings; discovers and annotates populations using shape-constrained trees, marker selection and sample-specific standardized thresholds. Clinical outcomes enter downstream association analysis, not as required cell-type labels for discovery. | Directly relevant to transcript **02:28–03:14**: multiple trees and selection of useful leaves are already part of this method. The paper evaluates simulations and immunotherapy cohorts, including cross-study phenotype evidence. It relies on one-dimensional modal structure and does not guarantee the shortest two- or three-marker phenotype. A priority direct interpretable-discovery comparator. |
| **CCAST**, 31 July 2014. [Paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25078380/) | Clusters unlabeled measurements first, then learns a classification/sorting tree and removes contaminating cells where needed. Selects markers, thresholds and order. | Breast-cancer cell-line and normal-bone-marrow examples assess homogeneity and signaling differences. Essential conceptual precedent for discovery followed by an explanation. Its univariate tree does not mean its upstream clustering only considered one variable at a time; filtering also makes retained-cell coverage relevant. |
| **FlowSOM**, online 8 January 2015. [Paper](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/cyto.a.22625) | Unlabeled multivariate measurements; self-organizing map plus metaclustering, with marker summaries/star plots. | Original evaluations include flow/mass cytometry and a bone-marrow example with manual-gating comparisons. Strong practical discovery baseline, not a small-marker gate generator. Its displayed minimum-spanning tree is not a serial marker decision tree. Any reference-derived choice of cluster count must be disclosed. |
| **PhenoGraph**, 2015. [Paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4508757/) | Unlabeled multivariate cells; neighbor graph and community detection yield populations. | The original work checks labeled healthy-bone-marrow populations using precision, recall and robustness, then studies AML phenotypes. Useful secondary discovery baseline; its graph partition does not directly supply short marker gates or a hierarchical gating rule. |
| **ConvexGating**, 18 September 2026. [Paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-77360-z) | Supplied target/non-target assignments, potentially from unsupervised clustering; selects marker pairs and learns successive convex polygon gates. | Tests purity, recall and F1 across cytometry/CITE-seq settings, with sorting and sequencing validation in selected applications. Closest recent gate-reconstruction comparator. It does not itself decide which unlabeled population ought to exist. Pair it with a fixed discovery method for an end-to-end comparison. |

Three additional references define the boundary of the proposed contribution; they need not all be in the first experiment:

- **GateFinder**, online 29 May 2018, [paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29850785/): given a target population, searches pairwise marker plots and builds serial polygon gates. Case studies include simplifying a 15-parameter HIV-associated population to a smaller phenotype while retaining an outcome association. Its two-markers-at-a-time explanation is close to the interface idea, but the target is supplied and pairwise enumeration is part of its search.
- **Hypergate**, 2019 issue, [paper](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/35/2/301/5042172): supplied population-of-interest membership leads to marker thresholds/hyperrectangular gates, optimized for yield/purity and F1. Evaluations include curated FlowCAP and bone-marrow datasets. It is a compact gate baseline, not evidence that its target population was discovered without an upstream label assignment.
- **UNITO**, 12 February 2025, [paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-56622-2): trained bivariate segmentation uses manual masks and a predefined gating hierarchy/marker pairs. The paper assesses flow and mass cytometry using gate agreement and population-frequency agreement. It is useful as a supervised gate-transfer reference, not a like-for-like unlabeled panel-discovery competitor; its title's human-level wording must not be generalized to this project.

“No human labels supplied” and “no labels used internally” are different. Clustering followed by a classifier can use generated target labels without manual biological labels. Conversely, evaluating only the gate-reconstruction stage on manual target labels is a supervised task and must be labeled accordingly.

## Recommended first public benchmark: FlowCAP normal donors

Use **FlowCAP_ND** as the first planned specimen-held-out benchmark, subject to a data/provenance audit before fitting. The Hypergate paper describes blood from 30 healthy donors. The benchmark authors' [preparation script](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lmweber/cytometry-clustering-comparison/master/data_preparation/prepare_data_FlowCAP_ND.R) explicitly retains a `sample` column, 30 samples, 10 measurement dimensions and seven manually gated populations. It preserves unassigned cells as `NA` and states that the raw files were already transformed, so an additional default arcsinh transform is not appropriate. Source filenames must still be matched to labels explicitly during import.

The published access trail is [the Weber–Robinson benchmark and its provenance](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/cyto.a.23030/full), [author analysis code](https://github.com/lmweber/cytometry-clustering-comparison), [curated collection FR-FCM-ZZPH](https://flowrepository.org/id/FR-FCM-ZZPH), [original normal-donor accession FR-FCM-ZZYZ](https://flowrepository.org/id/FR-FCM-ZZYZ), and [the cited Cytobank manual-label source](https://community.cytobank.org/cytobank/experiments/4329). The author script verifies the sample/label structure and published provenance. FlowRepository access timed out in this research session; the full downloadable archive and original gate-coordinate/workspace availability have **not** been verified here. A population-label column alone is insufficient to claim recovery of the experts' exact marker hierarchy.

A sensible first execution contract is:

1. Verify specimen/donor IDs, named measurement channels, label meanings, compensation/transformation history and the available manual gating documentation. Exclude label and sample-ID columns from discovery features. Preserve unassigned events and record coverage rather than silently deleting every difficult cell.
2. Freeze donor-level discovery, selection and final-evaluation groups. Use a bounded per-donor cell sample for offline candidate generation, then evaluate frozen rules on the complete held-out specimens. Keep all cells/replicates from one donor on the same side. The chosen donor allocation is an experimental design choice, not a result supplied by the papers.
3. Begin with **cytometree, FAUST and FlowSOM followed by ConvexGating** as the small comparator set. Compare their end-to-end agreement with the same expert reference. Separately, if testing explanation quality, give competing gate-reconstruction methods the same frozen target assignments; do not let different teachers masquerade as better gate fitting.
4. Report per-reference-population precision, recall and F1, macro summaries, population-frequency error per specimen, and ARI as an additional global summary. Report the number of markers per node and per complete path, gate/term count, coverage, runtime and rule transfer separately. Rare-population failures must not disappear behind an overall score dominated by common cells.
5. Prefer two-marker candidates, allow three-marker candidates when their held-out benefit justifies them, and label larger exact-rule alternatives explicitly. Show a small set of supported tradeoffs offline; log human choices. Preserve final evaluation data from both automatic tuning and repeated interactive inspection.

This is a practical starting recommendation, not a claim that FlowCAP_ND is the definitive dataset. For a second, gating-protocol-focused study, the HIPC standardized immunophenotyping experiment provides a documented multicenter setting and common panels/gating strategy; inspect its sample metadata and accessible workspace files first. [Finak et al., 10 February 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep20686). The existing two-donor Levine dataset remains useful for continuity, but by itself provides limited independent donor-level evidence.

## Implications for the lab's candidate-tree idea

The offline candidate-tree interface is consistent with expert practice, but discovering multiple trees is not a novelty claim by itself. The useful research target is whether the alternatives make reliable, compact marker-defined decisions easier to inspect and recover across specimens.

Do not rank a node by the maximum of incomparable density/graph scores suggested in transcript **03:14–06:04**. Preserve the actual partition and its separate evidence measures; make any cross-method selection rule explicit. Distinguish fixed-panel assignment stability, repeated selection of the same panel, recurrence of a marker subset, and recurrence of the biological population discussed at **06:04–12:32**. None alone proves that a split is meaningful. Also distinguish an automatic method's decision to stop from an expert's choice not to expand a displayed branch.

The first claim worth establishing is modest and useful: on new specimens, the method presents a small number of readable candidate gates or rules that recover documented populations with acceptable coverage and marker complexity. Claims of discovering new biology or outperforming expert gating require stronger evidence than that first benchmark supplies.
